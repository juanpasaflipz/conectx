# Conectx Peñas Go-To-Market Memo

## Point of the Plan

Conectx should not optimize for broad installs yet. The goal is dense, same-place, same-time adoption so the product can be tested under real stadium failure conditions before the World Cup on June 11, 2026.

The first wedge is organized Liga MX fan groups (peñas) in CDMX.

## Why This Wedge

Peñas are a better starting point than generic football fans because:

- They already move in groups.
- They already coordinate before, during, and after matches.
- Connectivity failures happen in the exact moments that matter most.
- One organizer can pull in 5 to 10 other users at once.
- Repeated match attendance creates a real feedback loop, not a one-off demo.

## User, Champion, and Core Job

**Primary user:** organized peñas of Liga MX teams in CDMX.

**First champion:** the younger tech-forward lieutenant who helps run the WhatsApp group.

Why this person:

- They already handle logistics.
- They are more willing to try a new tool than the top group leader.
- They can get adoption without needing full group consensus first.

**Core job to be done:**

When WhatsApp fails inside or outside the stadium, the group still needs one shared plan for how to regroup.

This means the product is not primarily "offline chat." The wedge product is:

**The peña's offline stadium plan that still works when signal dies.**

## Current Broken Workaround

Today peñas use one WhatsApp group plus pre-agreed meeting points such as "Gate X" or "Puerta Y."

This breaks when:

- mobile data collapses inside the stadium
- messages send late or not at all
- people split during halftime or after the final whistle

The worst moment is post-match exit chaos, when missing members need to regroup fast and the existing plan is no longer reliable.

## Wedge Product

Before entering the stadium, the organizer sets and shares a persistent plan that every member can keep locally on-device.

The minimum useful plan includes:

- primary meetup point
- backup meetup point
- regroup rules if people get separated
- optional subgroup labels if the peña splits up

The key onboarding ritual is simple:

**Before entering, each member joins the squad and saves the stadium plan locally.**

If that step feels heavier than repeating instructions in WhatsApp, adoption dies.

## Match-Day Test

The first field tests should optimize for one concrete outcome:

**After the final whistle chaos, can the peña find missing members and regroup at the meetup point in under 10 minutes?**

That is the core success metric for this wedge.

### Match-Day Checklist

Before match:

- organizer creates squad
- organizer sets primary and backup meetup points
- organizer adds regroup instructions
- each member joins the squad
- each member confirms the plan is saved locally

After match:

- note how many members got separated
- note whether mobile data failed
- note whether Conectx was actually used
- measure time to full regroup
- record what confused people or slowed them down

## Proof Artifact

The next lieutenant will not be convinced by analytics screenshots. The best proof is a social artifact from someone like them.

**Proof artifact:**

The group found its missing members and regrouped in under 10 minutes with no mobile data, shown as:

- a short video recorded right after the match
- a short voice note from the lieutenant explaining what happened

Capture the exact stadium, match, signal conditions, and regroup time.

## Distribution Plan

Do not try to recruit random users at scale yet.

The near-term loop is:

1. Recruit one lieutenant in one peña.
2. Help them set up one squad plan for one match.
3. Get 5 to 10 members to join before entry.
4. Observe whether regrouping works after the match.
5. Turn that result into proof for the next lieutenant.

The right target is not 1,000 installs. The right target is 3 successful peña tests with clear post-match evidence.

## Near-Term Product Priority

Product work should support the field test, not sprawl into generic social features.

Priority order:

1. Make squad join plus local plan save extremely easy.
2. Make the stadium plan obvious and persistent offline.
3. Make post-match regroup status easy to understand under pressure.
4. Remove any onboarding step that is not required for the field test.

## What Not to Optimize Yet

- broad monetization
- ads
- generic growth loops
- feature breadth for casual users
- polishing flows that do not help a peña regroup after a match

Monetization can come after dense match-day adoption proves that the product works in the real failure mode.
